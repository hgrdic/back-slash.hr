Real-time sync across three commerce platforms

BigCommerce

A Rails sync engine holding items, orders, customers and payments consistent across BigCommerce, Square and Clover in real time. Three systems with different data models, different rate limits and no shared source of truth.

The interesting part was the background-job architecture: ordering guarantees so events could not be applied out of sequence, retries that were safe to run twice, and idempotency throughout so a replayed webhook never double-charged or duplicated an order.

Ruby on Rails · background jobs · idempotency · third-party APIs

Publishing under a contractual 7-minute deadline

Digital signage network

An advertising company running screens across hotels and venues had a contractual obligation: any playlist, however many pages, had to be published to its displays within seven minutes. Large playlists were missing that window.

I sharded the rendering workload and fanned it out across AWS Lambda, scaling invocations with the size of the job and caching aggressively in Redis. Lambda code shipped as Docker images, with live traffic split across EC2 and Lambda targets behind an ALB using weighted routing. The deadline held.

Ruby on Rails · AWS Lambda · Redis · Docker · ALB

Ordering and loyalty on point-of-sale hardware

US sandwich franchise

A customer ordering application integrated deeply with Clover: orders arriving from the app had to print correctly on two different targets: a kitchen ticket for staff and a customer-facing receipt, with a loyalty programme running alongside.

Work of this kind is why I ended up building several native bridges between Android and the Clover SDK, plus hybrid Ionic and Cordova integrations, across multiple engagements in that ecosystem.

Android · Clover SDK · Ionic / Cordova · Rails
