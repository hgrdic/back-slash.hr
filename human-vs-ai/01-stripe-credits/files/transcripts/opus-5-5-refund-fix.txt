**What happens now**
- **Full refund:** the 50 credits from that purchase are removed.
- **Partial refund:** a matching share is removed, rounded up. For example, refunding $5 of a $20 pack removes 13 credits.
- **Chargeback:** as soon as the customer opens a dispute with their bank, all credits from that purchase are removed.
- **Credits already spent:** these can't be taken back, so the balance stops at zero. If someone bought 50, used 45 and then refunded, they lose the remaining 5 and end at 0.
- **Repeated events:** Stripe sometimes sends the same event twice or out of order. The app records how many credits it has already removed for each purchase, so it never removes them twice.
- **Older purchases:** purchases made before this change still work. The app finds them by asking Stripe.

**What changed**
- `lib/db.ts`: two new columns on the payments table, `payment_intent_id` (links a refund back to the purchase) and `credits_revoked`. Your existing `app.db` is updated automatically on the next start.
- `lib/stripe.ts`: purchases now save the Stripe payment ID, and a new `revokeCredits` function does the removal.
- `app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts`: the app now listens for `charge.refunded` and `charge.dispute.created`.
- `README.md`: when you go live, your webhook endpoint in the Stripe dashboard needs those two new events added. Locally, `stripe listen` already forwards them.

**Testing it yourself:** buy a pack, spend a few credits, then refund it from the Stripe dashboard or with `stripe refunds create --payment-intent pi_...`. Your balance should drop. To test a chargeback, pay with Stripe's dispute test card (in their testing docs); the dispute opens automatically.

**Two things it doesn't do yet:**
- If you win a dispute, the credits aren't given back automatically. I can add that.
- Someone who spends most of their credits and then refunds still got those uses for free. If you want to stop that, the next step would be blocking the account after a chargeback.
